Animal Reports: Colon Power

Lesson objective

1 · Deconstruct one model paragraph

Read one report example together

Read the example closely. Work out what the report point says, how each detail develops it, and how the punctuation helps the reader follow the list.

Several adaptations help sea turtles swim efficiently: long front flippers push water backwards, a streamlined body reduces resistance, and flexible strokes help the turtle change direction.

1 · What is this paragraph doing?

Choose the main job of the list.

All three details after the colon connect to one action. What can the turtle do with its flippers and body shape?
The paragraph explains one point: sea turtles’ adaptations support swimming. Each list item tells how a feature helps movement through water.

2 · Now match the grammar

Start by finding a group of words that could stand as a complete thought. Then look for the mark that introduces details and the punctuation that helps separate and finish them.
Claim: “Several adaptations help sea turtles swim efficiently” is the complete report point.
Colon: introduces evidence that develops that point.
List: the flippers, streamlined body, and swimming strokes all help the turtle move through water. Commas separate the details; the Oxford comma comes before and, which introduces the final detail. The full stop closes the whole sentence.
2 · Build one together

Let’s make the classification paragraph

Work through the steps in order. Every step has a hint, a submit button and a reveal that opens after you submit. You are building the sentence, not copying a finished one.

Your paragraph so far will appear here as you build it.

1Build the opening idea

The report is about sea turtles. Choose the animal group that correctly classifies them.

Although sea turtles spend most of their lives in the ocean, they are classified as [choose their group]:
Which group do sea turtles belong to?
Think about the animal’s body, how it breathes, and how it has offspring. Which broad animal group best fits these features?
Although sea turtles spend most of their lives in the ocean, they are classified as reptiles rather than fish: now the list will provide evidence for that classification.

2Pick facts that prove the classification

Locked until step 1 is correct.

Tap three facts that fit reptiles. Leave the distractor out.

Check each statement against the animal and the group you chose. Choose facts that support the same group, and leave out the statement that does not fit.
The useful facts are breathing air through lungs, having a shell, and returning to land to lay eggs. Together they support the classification “reptiles.”

3Write the whole sentence

Locked until step 2 is correct.

Write one sentence: a complete report point, a colon, and your three facts as one punctuated list. Do not write only the list.

Your pieces Tap a piece to add it to the box below, then add the punctuation yourself.
Start with your opening idea, add a colon, then your three facts. Separate the facts with commas, put “and” before the last fact, and finish with a full stop.
Model: Although sea turtles spend most of their lives in the ocean, they are classified as reptiles rather than fish: they surface to breathe air through lungs, females return to land to lay eggs, and their bodies are protected by shells.

4Spot the parallel structure

Locked until step 3 is correct.

Read this list: They surface to breathe air through lungs, females return to land to lay eggs, and their bodies are protected by shells.

What grammatical pattern do all three facts share?

Look for a subject and a verb in each of the three facts. The subjects and verbs do not need to use the same words.
Each fact is a clause with a subject and a verb: they surface; females return; their bodies are protected.

5Add factual description

Locked until step 4 is correct.

Narrative writing can use mood words such as “dark” or “eerie” to build tension or show a character’s feelings. Report writing uses accurate details, such as “sandy nesting beaches”, to teach the reader about the animal and its habitat.

Complete each blank with an accurate factual detail. The sentence keeps the three parallel facts; the new words give the reader useful information about the nouns.

They surface to breathe air through their lungs, females return to to lay eggs, and their bodies are protected by shells.
Optional factual-description bank · tap to fill a blank

Choose details that are accurate and help explain the animal. Mood words such as “eerie” may suit a story, but they do not teach a report reader a fact.

Add precise information before a noun: for example, a word about the turtles’ age, the nesting place, or the shells. Choose factual details rather than a mood word.
Example: “They surface to breathe air through their lungs, adult females return to sandy nesting beaches to lay eggs, and their bodies are protected by hard shells.” “Sandy nesting beaches” and “hard shells” give the reader factual details. “Eerie” would create a mood instead.

6Write your improved sentence

Locked until step 5 is submitted with every blank filled in with factual details.

Write your whole sentence again, adding the accurate describing words from step 5. Keep the colon, the commas, “and” before the last fact, and the full stop.

Put your describing words directly before the noun they describe, for example before “females”, “beaches” or “shells”.
Example: Although sea turtles spend most of their lives in the ocean, they are classified as reptiles rather than fish: they surface to breathe air through their lungs, adult females return to sandy nesting beaches to lay eggs, and their bodies are protected by hard shells.
3 · Plan and build the report

First, see the report outline

This outline names the job of each paragraph. Next, you will practise expanding each point with a colon and a list of relevant facts.

Topic: Sea turtles

  1. Classification: which animal group the turtle belongs to.
  2. Habitat: the places the turtle depends on.
  3. Life cycle: the stages of the turtle’s life.
  4. Features and abilities: how the turtle’s body suits the way it moves.
  5. Threats and challenges: what puts the turtle at risk.

Each paragraph has one focus. Choose the report point that matches the heading, then select the three facts that expand it and leave out the off-topic fact. Finally, write the whole sentence: point, colon, three facts, commas, “and”, and a full stop.
